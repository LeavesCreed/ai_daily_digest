IBM's approach — using AI to defend the open source supply chain rather than just attack it — represents a structural countermeasure at a time when 90% of Fortune 500 companies depend on OSS.

### Key Technical Points
- 20,000+ engineers augmented by frontier AI for vulnerability detection and remediation

Andrej Karpathy's Auto-Research project uses agent swarms to train LLMs on incremental self-improvement at GPT-2 scale. ### Why It Matters
The shift from AGI to RSI reflects a maturing understanding that "can AI improve itself" is both more measurable and more consequential than "can AI think like humans." AutoScientists demonstrates that agent teams can independently discover improvements beyond human-designed baselines — a concrete RSI milestone that moves the concept from speculation to reproducible results.

### Key Technical Points

- 74.4% mean leaderboard percentile on BioML-Bench (24 tasks; +8.33% over prior best AI agent)
- 1.9x faster GPT training optimization; discovered 7 improvements where single-agent found 0

- Karpathy's Auto-Research: agent swarms training GPT-2 scale models iteratively

# Important Paper of the Day

## AutoScientists: Self-Organizing Agent Teams for Long-Running Scientific Experimentation

This paper introduces AutoScientists, a decentralized multi-agent system where AI agents autonomously propose, execute, and iterate on scientific experiments over extended time horizons. On BioML-Bench (24 tasks spanning biomedical imaging, protein engineering, single-cell omics, and drug discovery), AutoScientists achieves 74.4% mean leaderboard percentile, outperforming the strongest single AI agent by 8.33%. On GPT training optimization, it discovers improvements 1.9x faster and finds 7 optimizations where single-agent baselines find none. On ProteinGym, it discovers a novel ACE2-Spike binding method with +12.5% Spearman correlation over the state-of-the-art. This represents a concrete advance in recursive self-improvement — agents that independently push beyond human-designed baselines.

Link:
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28655v1
