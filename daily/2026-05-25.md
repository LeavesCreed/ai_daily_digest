## 1. AI Hallucinations Infiltrate Academic Research: Lancet Study Finds 4,000+ Fabricated References

### What Happened
A study published in The Lancet audited nearly 2.5 million biomedical papers and 97 million citations on PubMed Central. Researchers led by Columbia's Maxim Topaz found over 4,000 fabricated references embedded across nearly 3,000 papers. The rate of fake references grew from 1 in 2,828 papers in 2023 to 1 in 458 in 2025. In the first seven weeks of 2026, the rate reached 1 in 277 papers.

### Why It Matters
Fabricated citations in biomedical literature directly compromise the evidence chain that informs clinical guidelines and treatment decisions. A hallucinated reference at the bottom of a citation stack propagates upward through systematic reviews and into clinical practice. This is not a theoretical concern — Topaz notes that paper mill articles have already been found in systematic reviews informing clinical guidelines.

### Key Technical Points
- 4,000+ fabricated references in ~3,000 papers across 97M citations
- Growth rate: 12x increase from 2023 to 2025 (1/2,828 to 1/458)
- 2026 Q1 rate: 1 in 277 papers contains at least one fake reference
- AI tools are the primary driver: fake sourcing went "vertical" in 2024
- Published in The Lancet (May 2026)

## 2. AV2 Codec Confirmed for Release on May 29 After Years of Development

### What Happened
The Alliance for Open Media's AOM Video Model (AVM), the reference software implementation of the AV2 codec, has been bumped to version 1.0.0 with the changelog pre-dated for May 29, 2026. This confirms the imminent release of AV2 as the open-source, royalty-free successor to AV1. VideoLAN recently released Dav2d, an open-source AV2 decoder, further signaling ecosystem readiness.

### Why It Matters
AV2 represents the next generation of royalty-free video compression. With better compression efficiency than AV1, it directly impacts streaming costs, video storage, and bandwidth utilization for every internet video platform. The release timing ahead of Computex 2026 suggests hardware ecosystem support announcements are imminent.

### Key Technical Points
- AVM reference software bumped to v1.0.0
- Release date set for May 29, 2026
- Dav2d (open-source AV2 decoder) already published by VideoLAN
- Successor to AV1 with improved compression efficiency
- Timing aligned with Computex 2026 for hardware announcements

## 3. Disney, Universal, Warner Bros. Win Key Ruling Against Hailuo AI Over Copyright Infringement

### What Happened
A federal judge in California refused to dismiss copyright claims brought by Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros. against the makers of Hailuo AI (MiniMax). The studios argued that Hailuo AI's ability to generate near-perfect likenesses of their well-known characters constitutes direct and secondary copyright infringement. The court found the studios' claims plausible.

### Why It Matters
This ruling establishes an early judicial framework for AI-generated character replication. The court rejected MiniMax's arguments that the models function differently than alleged, setting a precedent that AI systems capable of reproducing trademarked character likenesses may face direct liability. As an early 2026 ruling, it will influence how generative media companies approach IP compliance.

### Key Technical Points
- Defendants: Shanghai Xiyu Jizhi Technology (MiniMax) and Nanonoble Pte
- Claims: Direct and secondary copyright infringement
- Studios: Disney, Universal, Warner Bros., and subsidiaries
- Hailuo AI generates near-perfect character likenesses from text prompts
- Case advances to discovery phase
