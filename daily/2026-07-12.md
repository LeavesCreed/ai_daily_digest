## 1. Meta Reins In Muse Image After Instagram Privacy Backlash

### What Happened
Meta pulled a feature of its newly launched Muse Image model that automatically accessed public Instagram photos for AI image generation, following widespread privacy criticism. The move came less than a week after Muse Image's debut as Meta's first image-generation model.

### Why It Matters
The backlash highlights the growing tension between AI training data needs and public data rights. The speed of Meta's retreat signals that even the largest platforms cannot ignore user consent concerns in the AI era.

### Key Technical Points
- Muse Image is Meta's first in-house image generation model
- The controversial feature automatically pulled public Instagram images as training/reference data
- Meta reversed course within days of launch due to public outcry
- Sets precedent for how social media platforms handle user content for AI training

## 2. SK Hynix Debuts on Nasdaq With $26.5B IPO, Largest Foreign US Listing

### What Happened
SK Hynix, the world's leading HBM memory manufacturer, debuted on Nasdaq raising $26.5B in the largest US listing by a foreign company and second-largest share sale in US history (after SpaceX's $86B IPO). Shares rose 12.8% on day one. The company's market value has surpassed $1 trillion.

### Why It Matters
The listing gives US investors direct access to the critical HBM memory supply chain. SK Hynix's monopoly-like position in HBM3E memory—essential for every NVIDIA AI GPU—makes this a strategic infrastructure asset going public.

### Key Technical Points
- $26.5B raised: largest foreign IPO in US history
- SK Group Chair Chey Tae-won says doubling production capacity is still not enough to meet demand
- Shares surged 630% over past 12 months, market cap >$1 trillion
- The "Korea Discount" may narrow as US investors gain direct access
- HSBC analysts estimate up to 20% valuation uplift from US listing

## 3. OpenAI Pivots to Families as ChatGPT User Base Ages

### What Happened
OpenAI is hiring a dedicated product manager for families, caregivers, and older adults. Sensor Tower data shows ChatGPT users aged 35+ rose to 31% (from 26%), while 18-24 share fell to 29% (from 34%). Nearly 1 in 4 US parents who are smartphone users used ChatGPT in Q2.

### Why It Matters
This signals OpenAI's strategic pivot from individual productivity tool to household platform—similar to how Google, Apple, and Meta evolved. But AI raises the stakes: safety-by-design for children and teens requires fundamentally different product architecture.

### Key Technical Points
- Dedicated PM role for families, caregivers, and older adults
- ChatGPT 35+ user share: 26% -> 31% YoY
- 18-24 user share: 34% -> 29% YoY
- 38% of children reported using generative AI vs 27% of parents aware
- Family Online Safety Institute calls for age-appropriate safeguards, content controls, and parental oversight

## 4. Trump AI Export Promotion Program Gets Only 78 Applications

### What Happened
The Commerce Department's American AI Exports Program received just 78 applications in its first round. Companies are wary of participating due to the administration's abrupt, since-reversed order forcing Anthropic to cut off foreign model access, undermining the program's credibility.

### Why It Matters
The program's underwhelming launch reveals a fundamental contradiction in US AI policy: the administration simultaneously wants to promote US AI exports globally while pursuing ad-hoc national security restrictions that make foreign customers question reliability.

### Key Technical Points
- 78 applications in first round, far below expectations
- Companies cite "headache and complexity" of tying projects to Trump administration
- The Anthropic Fable 5 export ban (later reversed) damaged trust in US AI supply reliability
- Highlights tension between export promotion and national security control

## 5. Broadcom Lands $30B Apple Deal for Custom AI Server Chips

### What Happened
Broadcom disclosed in a securities filing that it agreed to supply Apple with custom ASIC silicon for multiple generations of Apple products, in a multiyear deal expected to exceed $30 billion. Bloomberg reports Broadcom is working with Apple on a specialized AI server chip for data centers.

### Why It Matters
This deal cements Broadcom's position as the leading custom AI chip designer outside of NVIDIA's GPU monopoly. Apple's move toward custom AI silicon mirrors the industry trend of hyperscalers building in-house inference infrastructure.

### Key Technical Points
- $30B+ multiyear deal for custom ASIC silicon
- Includes specialized AI server chip for Apple data centers
- Builds on existing Broadcom-Apple relationship
- Apple joins Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft in custom AI chip development
- Signals accelerating vertical integration in AI inference infrastructure

# Important Paper of the Day

## BabyFlow: 3D Modeling of Realistic and Expressive Infant Faces

Published in Nature Scientific Reports. BabyFlow is a generative AI model that disentangles facial identity and expression in infant faces, enabling independent control over both. It uses cross-age expression transfer from adult 3D scans to enrich infant datasets. Improves 3D reconstruction accuracy in highly expressive regions (mouth, eyes, nose) and supports synthesis and modification of infant expressions while preserving identity.

Link:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-61298-9
