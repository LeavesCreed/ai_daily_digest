## 1. Anthropic Hits First Profit, Commits $1.25B/Month to SpaceX for Compute

### What Happened
Anthropic is on track for its first quarterly operating profit, projecting $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue — more than doubling from $4.8 billion in Q1 — with $559 million in operating income. The growth is driven by Claude Code, whose run-rate revenue has exceeded $2.5 billion. Simultaneously, Anthropic signed a compute agreement to pay SpaceX $1.25 billion per month through May 2029 for access to Colossus and Colossus II data centers (300+ MW, 220,000+ NVIDIA GPUs).

### Why It Matters
This marks a structural shift in AI economics. Anthropic's compute cost per dollar of revenue dropped from $0.71 to $0.56 QoQ, proving operating leverage is achievable at scale. The SpaceX deal cements compute access as the primary strategic bottleneck — more valuable than model architecture advantages. Claude Code alone has become a $2.5B product, confirming agentic coding as the killer app for LLMs.

### Key Technical Points
- Q2 2026 revenue: $10.9B (130% QoQ growth)
- First profitable quarter: $559M operating income

- Claude Code run-rate: $2.5B+, doubled since start of 2026

## 2. NVIDIA Vera CPU Opens $200B Agentic AI Market

### What Happened
NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang announced the Vera CPU as "the world's first CPU purpose-built for agentic AI," claiming it opens a brand new $200 billion TAM. Vera is sold standalone and bundled with the Rubin GPU. Huang disclosed $20 billion in standalone Vera CPU sales already booked this year. The announcement came during NVIDIA's earnings call, where the company reported $81.6B quarterly revenue with a $91B Q3 forecast.

### Why It Matters
Vera represents NVIDIA's strategic expansion beyond GPU dominance into the CPU market, directly competing with Intel, AMD, and AWS's homegrown AI CPUs. Huang's thesis — that agents will run primarily on CPUs while models think on GPUs, and that billions of agents will each need their own CPU — redefines the compute architecture for the agentic era.

### Key Technical Points
- Vera: First CPU designed specifically for agentic AI workloads
- Optimized for token processing throughput, not multi-instance app concurrency
- $20B in standalone Vera sales already booked in 2026
- $200B claimed new addressable market
- NVIDIA Q2 FY27 revenue: $81.6B (record), Q3 guidance: $91B

## 3. GitHub Confirms Breach of 3,800 Internal Repos via Poisoned VS Code Extension

### What Happened
GitHub confirmed that approximately 3,800 internal repositories were breached after an employee installed a poisoned Nx Console VS Code extension (version 18.95.0). The malicious extension was live on the Visual Studio Marketplace for 11-18 minutes. The attack is attributed to threat group TeamPCP, which exfiltrated credentials and source code. The compromised extension was published using a stolen contributor's GitHub token via a malicious orphan commit.

### Why It Matters
This is the highest-profile software supply chain attack of 2026, directly compromising the largest code hosting platform. The attack chain demonstrates that IDE extensions have become critical attack surfaces. TeamPCP has previously breached OpenAI, the European Commission, and hundreds of other organizations through similar npm/PyPI supply chain attacks.

### Key Technical Points
- Malicious: Nx Console 18.95.0, live 11-18 minutes
- Stolen GitHub contributor token used for malicious orphan commit
- 3,800 internal GitHub repositories accessed
- TeamPCP threat group: also compromised OpenAI, EU Commission
- Linked to broader TanStack npm/PyPI "Mini Shai-Hulud" campaign

## 4. OpenAI Adopts C2PA and SynthID for Image Verification

### What Happened
OpenAI announced C2PA conformance and integration of Google DeepMind's SynthID invisible watermark for images generated by ChatGPT, Codex, and its API. The company also launched Verify, a public tool that lets users upload images and check for C2PA metadata and SynthID watermarks to determine AI origin.

### Why It Matters
Adopting C2PA and SynthID across OpenAI's product ecosystem establishes a multi-layered content provenance standard at the industry's largest scale. SynthID's invisible watermark is significantly harder to remove than metadata-only signals. This move signals coordinated industry-wide movement toward verifiable AI content.

### Key Technical Points
- C2PA conformance: standardized metadata for AI content provenance
- SynthID: invisible pixel-level watermark (Google DeepMind)
- Verify: public web tool for checking image provenance
- Covers: ChatGPT, Codex, OpenAI API, DALL-E 3, Sora
- Google adding SynthID to Pixel 8/9/10 video recordings
- C2PA scanning being added to Gemini

# Important Paper of the Day

## OpenAI Model Disproves 80-Year-Old Erdos Unit Distance Conjecture

OpenAI's general-purpose reasoning model produced an original proof disproving Paul Erdos's 1946 planar unit distance conjecture — the first time an AI has autonomously solved a prominent open problem central to a field of mathematics. The model discovered a novel family of dot arrangements that break Erdos's proposed limit, which mathematicians had believed for 80 years resembled square grids. The proof was validated by mathematicians including Noga Alon (Princeton), Melanie Wood, and Thomas Bloom. University of Toronto mathematician Arul Shankar stated the work "demonstrates that current AI models go beyond just helpers to human mathematicians — they are capable of having original ingenious ideas."
