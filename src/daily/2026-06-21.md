## 1. Nobel Laureate John Jumper Leaves DeepMind for Anthropic

### What Happened
John Jumper, who shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for AlphaFold, announced on June 20 that he is leaving Google DeepMind after nearly 9 years to join rival Anthropic. The same week, Character AI co-founder Noam Shazeer also left DeepMind for OpenAI. Jumper posted on X that DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis took a real chance letting him lead the AlphaFold team, and that the entire team taught him how to do great science.

### Why It Matters
This is a landmark event in the AI talent war. Jumper is a Nobel Prize winner and AlphaFold is one of the most impactful AI-for-science achievements. Bloomberg reports Jumper was a key member of Google's coding tools team — a core strength area for Anthropic (Claude Code). The exodus of top talent from DeepMind to Anthropic and OpenAI shows frontier AI lab competition has reached a new intensity.

### Key Technical Points
- John Jumper: 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, AlphaFold lead
- Anthropic recently released Claude Opus 4.8 and Mythos series, valued at $900B
- Noam Shazeer (Character AI co-founder) left DeepMind for OpenAI same week
- Bloomberg: Jumper was on Google's internal coding tools team, a product Google struggled to commercialize

## 2. IREN Limited AI Pivot: AI Revenue Up 839%, Lands $3.4B NVIDIA Deal

### What Happened
IREN Limited is transforming from a crypto mining company into an AI infrastructure provider. Q3 2026 AI revenue reached $33.6M, up 839% YoY and representing 23% of total revenue. The company secured a five-year, $3.4B cloud services deal with NVIDIA, increased secured power capacity to 5GW, and raised contracted ARR to $3.1B. GPU deployment targets for 2026 were upgraded.

### Why It Matters
IREN's transformation is a microcosm of the explosive growth in AI compute demand. The pivot from mining to AI infrastructure validates high-performance GPU compute as a new infrastructure asset class. NVIDIA's large contract commitment also signals that AI inference and training demand continues to expand rapidly, with cloud service models replacing self-built infrastructure.

### Key Technical Points
- AI revenue: $33.6M (Q3 2026), 839% YoY growth
- Five-year $3.4B cloud services agreement with NVIDIA
- 5GW secured power capacity, $3.1B contracted ARR
- Full transition from crypto mining to AI inference/training infrastructure

## 3. ChatGPT to Debut on Pentagon's GenAI.mil in Early July

### What Happened
OpenAI announced ChatGPT will launch on the Pentagon's GenAI.mil generative AI platform in early July, making it the latest model available on the platform. ChatGPT will be accessible to over 3 million defense personnel, certified for controlled unclassified information at Impact Level 5. OpenAI's latest model ChatGPT 5.4 was previously made available to the federal workforce via Amazon Bedrock and GovCloud.

### Why It Matters
This marks a significant milestone in OpenAI-DoD collaboration. While Anthropic's Claude Mythos faces US export controls, OpenAI is accelerating its defense sector entry, highlighting the complex landscape of AI company-government relationships. GenAI.mil deployment also signals that defense sector AI adoption is rapidly moving from experimentation to production.

### Key Technical Points
- Platform: GenAI.mil (Pentagon generative AI platform)
- Coverage: 3M+ defense personnel
- Security level: Controlled Unclassified Information, Impact Level 5
- ChatGPT 5.4 already available to federal workforce via AWS Bedrock/GovCloud

## 4. Meta AI App Quietly Strips Face-Recognition Code After WIRED Exposé

### What Happened
WIRED discovered dormant NameTag face-recognition libraries inside Meta's AI companion app, capable of creating local "faceprints." Meta pushed an update quickly after the report, removing nearly all traces of the code. With Meta AI app installs exceeding 50 million, these dormant biometric modules existed on millions of devices without any public launch announcement or user opt-in.

### Why It Matters
The incident raises serious privacy concerns in the AI era. NameTag modules existed on 50M+ installed devices without user knowledge. While Meta claims the feature was never enabled, the presence of the code itself demonstrates capability readiness. This could impact Meta's smart glasses strategy and invite regulatory scrutiny.

### Key Technical Points
- NameTag: face-recognition library for creating local faceprints
- Meta AI app: 50M+ installs
- Code stripped in June 2026 after WIRED discovery
- Never publicly enabled, but code presence is itself a privacy risk

## 5. Big Tech AI Infrastructure Debt Financing to Reach $570B in 2026

### What Happened
Morgan Stanley projects AI-linked global debt issuance will reach nearly $570 billion in 2026, with approximately $236 billion already sold by end of May. NVIDIA filed for investment-grade debt for the first time since 2021, upsizing from $20B to $25B after investors placed over $85B in orders. Meanwhile, the Federal Reserve under new Chair Kevin Warsh raised rates, significantly increasing borrowing costs for AI infrastructure.

### Why It Matters
AI infrastructure capex has shifted from a cash-flow story to a bond-market story. Tech giants find operating cash flow alone insufficient to sustain data center buildout pace. The $570B debt scale indicates unprecedented capital intensity in the AI arms race. Rising rates challenge the sustainability of this model — if rates continue climbing, project economics for some builds will be tested.

### Key Technical Points
- 2026 AI-linked global debt issuance: projected ~$570B
- NVIDIA: $25B bond offering (oversubscribed at $85B+)
- Fed rate hikes increase borrowing costs
- Tech giants turning to debt markets to fund AI data center expansion

# Important Paper of the Day

## Leiden Declaration: Mathematicians Unite to Protect Research Integrity in the AI Age

An international group of researchers from 15 universities across Europe and the US has developed the Leiden Declaration, endorsed by the International Mathematical Union. The declaration calls for protecting core values of mathematics in the age of AI. It does not call for banning AI but instead demands verifiability, transparency, and reproducibility of mathematical proofs. Key concerns include: AI-generated proofs that cannot be human-verified, inaccessibility of proprietary AI systems for academic research, and hallucinated citations affecting scholarly publishing.
