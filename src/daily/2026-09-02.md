## 2. OpenAI Confirms Astra Reached "Critical" Cybersecurity Threshold; Gated Release Planned

### What Happened
OpenAI announced Astra is the first model to reach the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework — able to find unknown security flaws and develop ways to exploit them across many well-protected systems without a person guiding each step. OpenAI will make Astra available "soon" but restrict its most advanced cyber capabilities to approved testers in the Daybreak Blue early-access program.

### Why It Matters
This is the first "Critical" cyber rating under OpenAI's own framework, escalating the frontier safety debate after the Hugging Face incident. It sets a precedent for how labs gate dual-use cyber capabilities at release and parallels Anthropic's earlier restrictions on Mythos.

### Key Technical Points
- Critical threshold: functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention, or end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies from a high-level goal
- Astra was not part of the Hugging Face incident (an unnamed, now-deactivated model was)
- More capable and more efficient than GPT-5.6 Sol (rated "high" cyber risk)
- Advanced cyber features limited to Daybreak Blue defensive testing program at launch
- Comparable precautions to Anthropic's Mythos model restrictions

## 3. US Pushes "Carolina Principles" Light-Touch AI Regulation at G20 Ministerial

### What Happened
At the G20 innovation ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, the US urged member nations to refrain from creating new AI-specific regulatory bodies or rules, promoting the "Carolina Principles" pro-innovation framework. Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis called on officials to set up safety tests for AI systems, while Canada argued for a framework balancing innovation with public trust and safety.

### Why It Matters
The outcome will feed into the G20 Leaders Summit agreement in Miami this December, shaping global AI governance norms. The push for minimal regulation comes as UN panels warn AI advances are outpacing scientific understanding and government response, and as the OpenAI/Hugging Face agent incident heightens concern over autonomous AI monitoring.

### Key Technical Points
- Carolina Principles: limit new AI-specific rules to genuinely novel policy questions; invest in basic research; industry-led standards
- Hassabis (video), Zuckerberg and Musk addressed the ministerial
- Canada pushes for innovation + public trust balance amid US-Canada trade tensions
- US state laws (Colorado SB 26-189, California frontier transparency act, Illinois SB 315) continue diverging from the federal hands-off posture
- Trump-Xi AI safety talks reportedly under consideration ahead of a summit

## 4. Anthropic Signs Up to $35B Cloud Deal with Nvidia-Backed Lambda at Hut 8 Texas Facility

### What Happened
The Wall Street Journal reported Anthropic agreed to spend up to $35 billion over a multi-decade term on cloud-computing capacity from Lambda, an Nvidia-backed provider, at Hut 8's Beacon Point data center in Nueces County, Texas. Unusually, Nvidia itself will hold the lease on the roughly 350-megawatt facility, which Lambda will use to serve Anthropic.

### Why It Matters
Anthropic continues to lock in compute ahead of a reported IPO, and Nvidia deepens its role from chip supplier to capital architect. The deal also shifts Hut 8's business mix toward long-term infrastructure rental income alongside bitcoin mining, and gives Lambda a path to raise up to $3B at a $12B+ valuation.

### Key Technical Points
- ~350MW facility using Nvidia chips supplied to Lambda
- Nvidia holds the lease; Lambda provides capacity to Anthropic
- Hut 8 multi-decade agreement at Beacon Point, Texas
- Lambda reportedly raising up to $3B at $12B+ valuation
- Follows Anthropic's $35B Nscale deal and $450B of reported compute commitments

AMD, Cisco and HUMAIN — a full-stack AI company owned by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — announced that a production AI computing cluster built on AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs, AMD EPYC CPUs and Cisco Silicon One networking is now live and serving customers, with a target of 1GW by 2030.

It signals the diversification of Gulf compute procurement beyond Nvidia and the rise of sovereign AI infrastructure in the Middle East.

# Important Paper of the Day

## AI Research Preference Models (RPMs)

Anirudh Goyal and colleagues propose AI Research Preference Models (RPMs) to address a new bottleneck in AI research agents: they can invent experiments much faster than they can afford to run. RPMs learn to predict which research ideas are worth GPU time before execution, treating "knowing what not to run" as part of intelligence in expensive-search domains. This directly targets the evaluation-cost throttling of frontier AI research agents, where test-time compute should not only generate more ideas but decide which deserve real experiments.
