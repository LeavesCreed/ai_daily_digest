## 1. US Government Orders Anthropic to Block Foreign Access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5

### What Happened
The Trump administration issued an unprecedented export control directive ordering Anthropic to suspend access to its latest AI models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, by any foreign national. Anthropic complied within 90 minutes, disabling global access. The "jailbreak" that triggered the ban was revealed to be a simple "fix this code" prompt that bypassed model guardrails. Over 100 cybersecurity executives and experts signed an open letter urging the administration to lift restrictions, warning the move could aid US adversaries.

### Why It Matters
This marks the first time the US government has used export control authorities to intervene in a deployed AI model, setting a precedent for future frontier model regulation. The incident exposes the fragility of current AI safety guardrails — a three-word prompt bypassed months of alignment work — and highlights the growing tension between AI capability release and national security concerns. The G7 response, with Mark Carney comparing the ban to the 2008 financial crisis over AI sovereignty, signals this is a structural shift.

### Key Technical Points
- "Fix this code" prompt bypassed Fable 5 safety guardrails, generating exploit scripts for known and novel CVEs
- Anthropic had received government approval for deployment before the ban
- 90-minute compliance deadline forced immediate global shutdown
- Amazon CEO Andy Jassy first raised concerns with White House after internal testing
- Export controls based on model outputs (what models can say) rather than deployment location

## 3. First Vision-Language Model Deployed in Orbit: NASA JPL and Loft Orbital Achieve Breakthrough

### What Happened
For the first time, an Earth observation satellite autonomously identified objects of interest without human analysts on the ground. The YAM-9 satellite, built by Loft Orbital, ran Google DeepMind's Gemma 3 vision-language model (VLM) onboard, processing natural language queries to classify sensor data in real time. The software package, NAVI-Orbital, was developed by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

### Why It Matters
This is the first reported use of a VLM in orbit, representing a fundamental shift in space-based sensing. Instead of downloading raw data for ground analysis, satellites can now perform initial data triage in orbit, dramatically reducing bandwidth requirements. Longer term, it proves the feasibility of running larger-scale AI infrastructure in space, with Loft targeting a 50-100 satellite constellation for real-time global coverage.

### Key Technical Points
- Gemma 3 VLM running on NVIDIA Jetson Orin AGX GPU in orbit
- Natural language queries like "monitor this border for suspicious activity" processed onboard
- NAVI-Orbital software streamlined to reduce library and memory footprint for space deployment
- YAM-9 launched Fall 2025 as pathfinder for orbital AI
- Planet Labs and Kepler Communications pursuing similar VLM deployments
