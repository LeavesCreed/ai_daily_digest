## 1. AI Data Centers Trigger Third Federal Grid Emergency in Six Months

### What Happened
On June 30, Energy Secretary Chris Wright signed two emergency orders under Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act — a 1935 wartime statute — directing PJM Interconnection, America's largest regional grid serving 67 million people across 13 states and D.C., to force AI data centers onto diesel backup generators within 15 minutes of an emergency signal. This is the third such invocation in 2026 alone, following a January cold snap and a May heat wave. PJM demand hit approximately 163 GW on July 2, narrowly below the all-time record. Day-ahead electricity prices spiked past $2,000/MWh. The orders remain in force through July 6.

### Why It Matters
This marks a structural crisis in AI infrastructure: data centers can be built in 18-24 months, but transmission lines take 3-7 years. Monitoring Analytics attributes 63% of PJM's capacity price increase to data center demand — translating to $9.3 billion in added annual costs. By 2028, the average PJM family could pay $70/month more. The emergency law was never designed to be used as standard practice. Texas Governor Abbott called for a ban on new data centers in rural areas; Senators Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez introduced the AI Data Center Moratorium Act.

### Key Technical Points
- DOE invoked Section 202(c) only ~29 times in 80+ years before 2026; 22 of those were during WWII
- PJM missed its reliability reserve target for the first time in December 2025
- Data centers consume 100-300 MW per facility; PJM projects 65 GW of new data center demand over next decade
- Diesel backup generators (Tier 2) operate under suspended EPA limits during emergencies
- University of Cambridge: data centers create localized heat islands up to 9.2°C above surrounding areas

## 3. NVIDIA AI Introduces ASPIRE: Self-Improving Robotics Framework

### What Happened
A team from NVIDIA, University of Michigan, UIUC, UC Berkeley, and CMU introduced ASPIRE (Agentic Skill Programming through Iterative Robot Exploration), a continual learning system that writes and refines robot control programs, then distills validated fixes into a reusable skill library. ASPIRE replaces coarse rollout feedback with per-primitive multimodal traces, allowing agents to localize failures to specific perception, planning, or control calls. On LIBERO-Pro, ASPIRE gains up to 77 points; on Robosuite, bimanual handover rises from 20% to 92%. Zero-shot transfer reaches ~31% on LIBERO-Pro Long tasks versus ~4% for prior methods.

### Why It Matters
ASPIRE addresses a fundamental scaling problem in robotics: existing coding agents discard fixes after each task, so the agent solving its hundredth task is no more experienced than its first. By accumulating a reusable skill library and using evolutionary search to escape local repair loops, ASPIRE demonstrates that code-as-policy systems can improve over time. Real-robot validation shows simulation-discovered skills transferred across different embodiments reduced debugging costs by ~10x.

### Key Technical Points
- Coordinator-actor architecture with shared skill library
- Per-primitive multimodal traces (RGB keyframes, grasp candidates, object poses, motion-planning results)
- Evolutionary search proposes K candidate programs per round
- Uses Claude Code with Claude Opus 4.6 and 1M-token context window
- Real-robot transfer: soda-can lifting improved from 13/20 to 19/20 using ~10x fewer tokens

## 4. Anthropic Cleared to Restore Mythos 5 Access to US Critical Infrastructure Organizations

### What Happened
Anthropic announced Friday that the US government had notified the company it could restore access to Mythos 5, its strongest cybersecurity model, for select US organizations that operate and defend critical infrastructure. This follows the June 12 Trump administration directive banning foreign nationals — including Anthropic employees — from accessing Fable 5 and Mythos 5. After two weeks of negotiations involving Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, Anthropic built a new safety classifier and industry jailbreak framework. The company continues working to expand Mythos 5 access and restore Fable 5 for general use.

### Why It Matters
This represents the first resolution of the landmark AI export control case that set a global precedent. The resolution — partial restoration with a new safety framework — establishes a template for how frontier model access may be managed at the intersection of national security and commercial deployment. EU officials, including French minister Benjamin Haddad, called for Europe to develop independent AI capabilities in response. Asian AI startups have already launched competing Mythos-like models.

### Key Technical Points
- Mythos 5: Anthropic's strongest cybersecurity model, redeployed to US critical infrastructure orgs
- New safety classifier and industry jailbreak framework built with Amazon, Microsoft, Google
- Fable 5 remains unavailable for general use; negotiations ongoing
- European response: calls for independent AI capability development

## 5. Foxconn Projects 30% Capex Growth Amid Robust AI Server Demand

### What Happened
Taiwan's Hon Hai Precision Industry (Foxconn) said it expects capital expenditure to grow by more than 30% in 2026, driven by sustained AI and cloud infrastructure demand. The company's Q2 2026 total sales reached NT$2.12 trillion ($67.4 billion), with its cloud and networking division contributing 48% of revenue. AI servers now account for more than half of Foxconn's total server revenue. The company reiterated its projection that sales would more than double in 2026.

### Why It Matters
Foxconn's capex guidance is a bellwether for the AI hardware supply chain. The 30% growth indicates that AI infrastructure buildout continues at an accelerating pace despite growing skepticism about AI lab economics. The fact that AI servers now constitute the majority of Foxconn's server business — the world's largest electronics manufacturer — signals that AI infrastructure spend is flowing through the entire manufacturing ecosystem.

### Key Technical Points
- 30%+ capex growth in 2026 driven by AI demand
- Q2 2026 sales: NT$2.12 trillion ($67.4B)
- Cloud/networking division: 48% of revenue
- AI servers: >50% of total server revenue
- Sales projected to more than double in 2026
