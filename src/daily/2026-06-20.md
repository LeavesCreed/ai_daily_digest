## 1. Nobel Laureate John Jumper Leaves Google DeepMind for Anthropic

### What Happened
John Jumper, the 2024 Nobel Prize winner in chemistry for co-creating AlphaFold, announced he is leaving Google DeepMind after nine years to join Anthropic. This follows Noam Shazeer, Google's Gemini co-lead, departing for OpenAI just days earlier. Jumper's team at DeepMind predicted over 200 million protein structures, cutting years off biological research. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis acknowledged Jumper's contributions in a public reply.

### Why It Matters
This is the highest-profile AI researcher departure from Google in 2026. The back-to-back exits of Jumper and Shazeer signal an accelerating talent drain from Big Tech to AI startups preparing for IPOs. Jumper brings world-class protein-folding and scientific AI expertise to Anthropic at a critical moment when the company is fighting a US government export ban on its frontier models. The move strengthens Anthropic's science and biology AI capabilities significantly.

### Key Technical Points
- Jumper won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry alongside Demis Hassabis
- AlphaFold has predicted over 200 million protein structures
- He served as VP, Engineering Fellow at Google DeepMind
- Anthropic is hosting a science event on June 30, likely related to Jumper's role
- This is the second major Google AI exit in one week after Noam Shazeer left for OpenAI

## 2. SpaceX Unveils AI1 Orbital Data Center Concept

### What Happened
SpaceX presented the AI1 concept, an orbital data center craft with interchangeable compute payloads. The system runs at 120kW average (150kW peak), powered by a 150kW solar array, with 110 square meters of deployable liquid radiators for cooling. The craft accepts hardware from multiple chip vendors in modular payload bays. Estimated platform efficiency is 70 kilowatts per ton, roughly equivalent to a single NVIDIA GB300 rack.

### Why It Matters
Orbital AI computing represents a radical departure from terrestrial data center architecture. If viable, it would bypass land, power, and water constraints that increasingly limit Earth-based AI infrastructure. However, major hurdles remain: thermal management in vacuum, radiation exposure, maintenance complexity, and launch costs. The concept also raises orbital debris concerns. SpaceX's proposal is the most technically detailed orbital compute concept to date.

### Key Technical Points
- 120kW average compute payload, 150kW peak
- 150kW solar array with 110 sq m liquid radiator cooling
- Modular chip-agnostic payload bays
- ~70 kW/ton platform efficiency
- Equivalent to one NVIDIA GB300 rack in power terms
- Covers thermal, micrometeoroid, and radiation protection

## 3. Amazon Explores External Sales of Trainium AI Chips

### What Happened
Amazon is in early-stage talks to sell its custom Trainium AI chips to third-party data centers, according to Bloomberg and TechCrunch. AWS AI chief Peter DeSantis confirmed the discussions, stating "there's so much underconsumption in AI" that external sales won't hurt cloud revenue. CEO Andy Jassy projected a potential $50B annual run rate for a standalone chip business in his April shareholder letter. Anthropic has committed up to 5 gigawatts and OpenAI about 2 gigawatts of Trainium capacity through AWS.

### Why It Matters
This directly challenges NVIDIA's near-monopoly on AI accelerator sales. Amazon's custom silicon business (Trainium, Graviton, Nitro) already crossed $20B annual run rate at triple-digit growth in Q1 2026. If Trainium becomes available externally, it would give infrastructure teams a cloud-tested, production-proven alternative to NVIDIA, potentially reshaping AI hardware economics. However, Trainium 3 is already sold out, raising allocation questions.

### Key Technical Points
- Custom silicon business at $20B+ annual run rate, triple-digit growth
- Trainium 3 largely sold out, demand building for gen-4
- Anthropic committed to 5 GW, OpenAI 2 GW of Trainium capacity
- Potential $50B standalone chip business per Jassy
- Early-stage discussions, no confirmed external customers yet

## 4. Anthropic Mythos Early Users Retain Access Despite US Export Ban

### What Happened
Despite the US government's export control order forcing Anthropic to shut down Fable 5 and Mythos 5 globally, approximately 200 organizations in the Project Glasswing cybersecurity collective retain access to Mythos Preview. Cisco confirmed continued access, and Dragos CTO Jon Lavender stated his company still has access. European cybersecurity agency ENISA, which was invited to join before the ban, was subsequently denied access. Commerce Secretary Lutnick's order relies on an unprecedented expansion of export control law covering mere usage of AI models.

### Why It Matters
This is the first real test of export controls on frontier AI models. The selective enforcement—allowing US-aligned organizations continued access while blocking others—creates a two-tier system for AI capability access. Legal questions about whether the US can dictate who uses AI models globally remain unresolved. The situation mirrors earlier encryption and spyware export control debates but with far higher stakes given AI's dual-use potential.

### Key Technical Points
- ~200 organizations retain Mythos Preview access via Project Glasswing
- Cisco confirmed continued access; Dragos confirmed
- ENISA denied access despite prior invitation
- Export order covers both foreign nationals abroad and inside US
- Lutnick's order expands export law from technology transfer to model usage
- Anthropic marketing Mythos as capable of "wreaking havoc" if released widely

## 5. Amazon Custom Silicon Hits $20B Run Rate, Memory Market Grows 4x

### What Happened
Amazon's custom silicon portfolio—Trainium, Graviton, and Nitro—crossed a $20 billion annual revenue run rate in Q1 2026 with triple-digit growth, per Bloomberg. Separately, the global memory chip market grew fourfold in 2026 driven by AI demand, particularly HBM memory for GPU accelerators. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy's April shareholder letter frames the chip business as potentially reaching $50B run rate if sold externally.

### Why It Matters
The numbers confirm that custom AI silicon has transitioned from experimental project to major revenue line. Amazon's $20B run rate rivals mid-tier semiconductor companies, and the trajectory toward $50B would make it a top-10 chip company globally. The 4x memory market growth underscores the insatiable hardware demand from AI training and inference workloads. These trends validate the thesis that AI infrastructure is becoming the largest capital expenditure cycle in technology history.

### Key Technical Points
- Amazon custom silicon: $20B+ annual run rate, triple-digit YoY growth
- Memory chip market: 4x growth in 2026, driven by HBM demand
- Jassy's $50B standalone chip business projection
- Trainium 3 sold out; gen-4 demand already building
- Anthropic (5 GW) and OpenAI (2 GW) as anchor Trainium customers

# Important Paper of the Day

## Self-Replicating AI Worm Using Open-Weight LLM on Single GPU (arXiv:2606.03811)

The University of Toronto CleverHans Lab published a paper demonstrating a self-replicating AI worm using an open-weight LLM running on a single GPU. The worm achieved 62% autonomous infection rate across 33 emulated enterprise network hosts over 7 days. This is the first publicly documented instance of an open-weight model being weaponized for autonomous network propagation, confirming long-standing security concerns about open-weight AI models. The paper underscores the urgency of AI supply chain security and has been cited in ongoing White House-Anthropic security framework negotiations.

Link:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03811
