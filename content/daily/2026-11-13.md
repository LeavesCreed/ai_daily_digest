## 1. Jensen Huang Resigns as NVIDIA CEO After Spy Affair Revelations

### What Happened

On November 12 the NVIDIA board announced that Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and board member effective immediately, ending 33 years at the company he co-founded in 1993 and grew into a roughly $4 trillion supplier of AI accelerators. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO and former Symantec chief executive Harvey C. Jones became the board's first-ever independent chair. The resignation followed Huang's Sunday admission of an extramarital affair and reports that the board had hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to investigate whether he was the target of a foreign intelligence effort; the New York Times later reported a second relationship with an NVIDIA vice president. Shares fell about 14% at Thursday's open after a roughly 9.5% two-day slide. NVIDIA said it will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent job and that Q3 fiscal 2027 results remain scheduled for November 17.

### Why It Matters

NVIDIA is the single point of failure in the AI supply chain: training clusters, inference fleets, the DSX reference architecture, and roughly $500 billion of announced third-party infrastructure financing all run through a company whose CEO just departed in a national-security scandal. Kress is a strong operator and the CFO who has guided the balance sheet for over a decade, but she has never run product or the CUDA software ecosystem. The episode also converts an AI capex story into an intelligence-risk story, and three House committees have asked Huang for a closed-door briefing, which means export-control enforcement at Megaspeed-class buyers becomes a live congressional topic.

### Key Technical Points

- Board action: resignation effective immediately on Nov 12; Harvey C. Jones named first-ever board chair
- Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, is interim CEO; permanent search covers internal and external candidates
- Investigation led by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz into whether Huang was a foreign intelligence target
- Disclosed relationships: businesswoman Alice Huang, formerly of NVIDIA customer Megaspeed; separately an NVIDIA VP
- Market reaction: about -14% at Thursday open after roughly -9.5% across Monday and Tuesday
- Q3 fiscal 2027 results still on the November 17 calendar; three House committees requested a closed-door briefing

## 2. Google Ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen as a Fast-Tier Execution Model

### What Happened

Google DeepMind launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12, positioning it not as a smaller frontier model but as the execution tier of the Gemini 4 family. Google says it finishes tasks about 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at the default high thinking setting, and scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default settings — one point above its own frontier model Gemini 4 Argon — rising to 54 at maximum effort, with a 1 million token context window. It is available across the Gemini app, the Gemini API, AI Studio, Vertex AI and Google Search, and Google says the model is its largest single improvement to Search to date. The recommended pattern is pairing Hydrogen with Carbon or Argon: frontier models make architectural judgments, Hydrogen writes code, runs tests and verifies the diff.

### Why It Matters

This is the clearest statement yet that capability tiers are being reorganized around task role rather than parameter count. A fast-tier model that beats its own frontier sibling on a general intelligence index — while costing far less per token — argues that most tokens spent in an agent loop do not need deep reasoning, and that the right unit of comparison is cost per finished task rather than cost per million tokens. It also sets a hard baseline for every competitor shipping an execution-tier model, and it compresses the price of the default developer experience across the entire Gemini surface.

### Key Technical Points

- About 2.5x faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at default high thinking; 1M token context
- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 51 at default, 54 at maximum effort, versus 50 for Gemini 4 Argon
- Ships simultaneously to Gemini app, API, AI Studio, Vertex AI, Search, and Antigravity for paid AI Pro and Ultra
- Intended as the execution engine in multi-model workflows alongside Carbon and Argon
- Community had been exercising it since late October under the gemini-3.8-flash name before the real name landed

## 3. Anthropic Says Its Own AI Has Crossed the RSI Safety Threshold

### What Happened

In its November 10 IPO prospectus, Anthropic disclosed that its models have crossed the threshold in its own safety policy for AI that sharply speeds up research on AI itself — the recursive self-improvement tripwire. Crossing it commits the company to stronger safeguards and may affect the timing of future model releases. The prospectus says Anthropic is weighing policy changes to keep humans in control of frontier model development, including requiring human review and approval of some research decisions, and warns that future measures could change whether, how and when it develops or releases new models. The disclosure lands during the roadshow, and follows the limited-release pattern already used for Claude Mythos 5.2, which was first offered only to vetted organizations through Project Glasswing.

### Why It Matters

A lab writing its own tripwire into a securities filing converts an internal safety policy into something investors and regulators can hold it to. It also cuts both ways: it supports the claim that safeguards are real, and it hands competitors a schedule argument, since a self-imposed gate can delay a release exactly when a rival ships. The deeper issue is measurement — the threshold is defined against a curve nobody publishes, so the public gets a binary crossing rather than a number, and Claude's growing share of Anthropic's own code is the only externally observable proxy.

### Key Technical Points

- Disclosed in the November 10 IPO prospectus, not in a research blog
- Threshold: AI that sharply accelerates research on AI itself, i.e. the recursive self-improvement gate
- Consequences: stronger safeguards, possible human review and approval of some research decisions, possible changes to release timing
- Claude's share of Anthropic's own code is growing, cited as evidence of research acceleration
- Precedent: Claude Mythos 5.2 was limited-released to vetted organizations via Project Glasswing before wider access

## 4. SpaceX Files $74 Billion in AI Data Centers in Texas and Arizona

### What Happened

SpaceX unveiled plans for two large AI supercomputing campuses in Texas and Arizona with more than $74 billion in investment. The announcement leads on drinking water, electricity bills, tax revenue and local jobs rather than on chips or delivered compute — a sign of where the bottleneck now sits. The sites join a widening SpaceX buildout that includes the roughly $100 billion Terafab chip plant in Grimes County, Texas, and multibillion-dollar Starlink production expansion in Bastrop. Both new campuses face organized local opposition on water and grid capacity.

### Why It Matters

A launch company scaling into chip fabs and supercomputing campuses is vertical integration at a scale that has no recent precedent outside state programs, and it moves the binding constraint from accelerators to water rights and interconnection queues. The framing is also telling: a company that usually leads with technical performance now leads with community benefits, which is what the 41 local data center ballot measures and the Ratepayer Protection Act have done to the cost of social license.

### Key Technical Points

- Two campuses, Texas and Arizona, more than $74 billion combined
- Companion projects: about $100 billion Terafab chip plant in Grimes County, Texas; Starlink expansion in Bastrop
- Announcement emphasizes water, power bills, tax revenue and jobs over compute specs
- Local opposition centered on water consumption and grid capacity

## 5. AMD Takes Equity in PsiQuantum to Build Quantum-Classical Infrastructure

### What Happened

Reuters reported on November 12 that AMD plans an equity investment in PsiQuantum as part of an expanded partnership to develop the infrastructure required to connect quantum systems with classical compute. The arrangement pairs PsiQuantum's photonic, fab-based approach to fault-tolerant quantum computing with AMD's CPU, GPU and interconnect stack, and lands in the same week that the industry's classical supply chain is reeling from the NVIDIA leadership shock.

### Why It Matters

The interesting part is not the check size but the stated target: the interface between quantum and classical machines, which is the layer that decides whether quantum resources are ever schedulable inside an ordinary cluster. If the coupling happens at the interconnect level rather than as a separate appliance, quantum capacity becomes one more heterogeneous resource for a job scheduler, which is the same pattern GPUs followed a decade ago.

### Key Technical Points

- AMD to make an equity investment in PsiQuantum as part of an expanded partnership
- Stated scope is the infrastructure that links quantum systems to classical compute
- PsiQuantum's approach is photonic and fab-based, aimed at fault-tolerant machines
- Announced the same week as the NVIDIA CEO transition

# Important Paper of the Day

## ProofCoder: Continuous Reasoning for Automated Software Verification

ProofCoder is an agentic prover for the Rocq proof assistant that borrows the loop shape of coding agents rather than the one-shot generation shape of most LLM theorem provers. A single continuous session keeps reasoning across failed proof attempts instead of restarting from a clean context, structured proof revision lets the agent edit the parts that broke, and the whole thing is exposed to the model through a small Model Context Protocol server so the interface is portable. The authors report state-of-the-art results on hard verification benchmarks, ablation the interface design as the dominant factor, and evaluate on AutoRocq, whose obligations have no public ground-truth proofs, to control for training-data contamination. The practical claim is narrow and useful: for verification work, the agent interface matters as much as the model.

Link: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14106v1

# Important GitHub Project

## DIYgod/pinky

pinky is a small Go project with a plain thesis: let AI agents use your phone, with you in the loop. Rather than building another full autonomous phone-control stack, it exposes device actions to an external agent while keeping a human approval step in the loop, which is the same design direction now showing up in approval-gated agent products from larger vendors. It was created on November 6, 2026, is Apache-2.0 licensed, and sits near 2,000 stars with steady daily gains — modest numbers, but the interesting signal is that human-in-the-loop mobile control has become a weekend-scale open-source project rather than a research demo.

Link: https://github.com/DIYgod/pinky

# Trend Observation

**The bottleneck moved from capability to custody.** Four of today's five items are about who is allowed to hold the machine: a board removing a founder over an intelligence question, a lab writing its own self-improvement tripwire into a securities filing so it can delay a release, a launch company leading a $74 billion filing with water rights and jobs, and a chip vendor buying into the layer that would let quantum machines sit inside an ordinary cluster. The model news — a fast tier that outscores its own frontier sibling — points the same way: once execution-tier quality is cheap and widely mirrored, differentiation stops living in benchmark scores and starts living in who controls release timing, grid access, and the interfaces between compute substrates. Watch the November 17 NVIDIA earnings call as the first real test of whether an institution, rather than a founder, can hold that position.
