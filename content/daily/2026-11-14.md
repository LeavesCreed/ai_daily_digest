## 1. Thinking Machines Opens Inkling 2 Small Weights After a Hosted-Only Week

### What Happened

On November 12 Thinking Machines Lab published downloadable weights for Inkling 2 Small, ending the model's first week as a hosted-only offering on its Tinker platform. The model is now on Hugging Face alongside additional API providers and serving and training integrations; Together AI lists it under Apache 2.0 at 0.30 dollars per million input tokens and 1.00 dollar per million output tokens. Inkling 2 Small is a 48-layer decoder-only transformer with a sparse mixture-of-experts feed-forward backbone, routing each token to 8 of 384 experts, with hybrid local and global attention and a vision transformer that projects images into the same hidden space as text. It carries 594 billion total parameters with 17 billion active per token and a one million token context window. Thinking Machines reports 30.6 to 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a large jump over the July Inkling Small, which had 276 billion total and 12 billion active parameters. At launch the lab said weights would follow external safety testing and red-teaming; the publication update does not report those findings.

### Why It Matters

This is the strongest Western open-weights release of the quarter measured by base-model quality per training FLOP: third-party reviewers report a base loss on held-out code below Kimi K3.1, for a model trained in a few dozen days. The interesting part is the sequencing. A US lab adopted the same "closed first, open later" pattern that Chinese labs used through 2026, but released the weights without publishing the red-team report that was supposed to gate them, which quietly converts a safety gate into a marketing beat. For buyers, Apache 2.0 at 594B total means the customization path (fine-tune on proprietary data, self-host when serving costs justify it) is now available from a US vendor at Chinese-open-weight price points.

### Key Technical Points

- Architecture: 48-layer decoder-only transformer, sparse MoE routing each token to 8 of 384 experts, hybrid local and global attention, native image input through a vision transformer
- Parameters: 594B total, 17B active per token, versus 276B total and 12B active for the July Inkling Small
- Context window: one million tokens; numerics support MXFP8 and NVFP4
- Third-party index: 30.6 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, Vals index around 56 percent
- Price: 0.30 dollars per million input tokens, 1.00 dollar per million output tokens on hosted providers
- License: Apache 2.0 per third-party listings; local inference and post-training recipes published alongside
- Verification gap: external red-teaming was announced as a precondition and its results are still unpublished

## 2. FBI Issues FLASH on ARTEX, an Open-Source Agentic Intrusion Framework

### What Happened

The FBI released a FLASH advisory on November 13 covering the tactics, techniques, procedures and indicators of compromise associated with ARTEX, an open-source large-language-model-driven agentic platform that automates multiple phases of a cyber intrusion at machine speed. The bureau states that since at least August 2026 a variety of malicious actors have used ARTEX in campaigns against organizations worldwide across financial services, government and technology, and that the framework sharply reduces the time needed to chain techniques, letting actors with minimal technical expertise run complex multi-target operations simultaneously. The advisory ships with mitigations. ARTEX already appeared in incident reporting this autumn: Microsoft attributed a ConnectWise backup compromise to an actor using it, Korean financial-sector intrusions were linked to it, and CrowdStrike described instances configured to run on DeepSeek V4.1-Flash with other sessions on GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6.

### Why It Matters

Attribution and tooling have separated. The same open-source harness is now reused across financially motivated criminals and state-linked groups, which means the defensive question is no longer which actor is coming but which console they rented. Because ARTEX orchestrates reconnaissance, exploitation and lateral movement rather than a single exploit, signature-based detection degrades: the observable is a sequence of ordinary tool calls at unusual tempo. The FLASH also sets an expectation for enterprises, that an agentic intrusion framework is a named threat category with published IOCs, the same status a botnet family would have.

### Key Technical Points

- Subject: ARTEX, an open-source LLM-driven autonomous penetration testing framework, used to automate multiple intrusion phases
- Timeline: in use by multiple malicious actors since at least August 2026, per the FBI
- Sectors observed: financial services, government, technology, across multiple regions
- Effect: compresses the time to chain techniques and lowers the skill floor for multi-target campaigns
- Prior reporting: Microsoft linked a ConnectWise backup compromise to ARTEX; CrowdStrike described instances running DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6
- Output: FLASH includes TTPs, indicators of compromise and mitigation guidance for agentic SI frameworks generally

## 3. Reuters Finds 23 Billion Yuan of Chinese Compute Projects Still Designed Around Restricted Nvidia Parts

### What Happened

Reuters reported on November 13 that a review of more than 150 Chinese procurement notices, investment plans, construction contracts and corporate disclosures found over 23 billion yuan, about 3.2 billion dollars, of computing projects and equipment agreements designed around Nvidia processors restricted by US export controls. The total spans planned investment, signed contracts and completed installations, and covers buildings and power systems rather than chips alone. Examples include a 6.2 billion yuan center in Xianyou county, Fujian, that specifies H100 chips; a 3 billion yuan Shaanxi project tied to a Sinomach subsidiary specifying H100 and H200; a 2.26 billion yuan Jilin project mixing H100 with domestic chips and expecting bank loans for about 80 percent of investment; a 1.5 billion yuan Inner Mongolia development proposing 625 H200 servers; and a 1.16 billion yuan Gansu contract planning roughly 3,072 H100 and H200 processors. Some facilities already run restricted hardware, including an H800 cluster in Jiangsu reported in March 2025. The documents do not establish whether processors were delivered, and the projects are not linked to Megaspeed or to the allegations around Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia chief executive on November 12.

### Why It Matters

Export control enforcement has a design-versus-delivery gap, and this is a measurement of the design side. Nvidia has said controls effectively shut it out of China's AI data-center market and excludes China data-center revenue from guidance, yet Chinese buyers keep writing purchase specs around the same part numbers, which implies either gray channels or a standing bet that controls loosen. Either way the policy question moves from who bought the chips to who operates the installed capacity and for what, an end-use problem that license review of individual H200s does not answer.

### Key Technical Points

- Scope: 150+ procurement notices, investment plans, construction and disclosure documents reviewed
- Aggregate: over 23 billion yuan, roughly 3.2 billion dollars, across planned, contracted and installed projects
- Named designs: H100 in Fujian and Shaanxi, H100 with domestic accelerators in Jilin, 625 H200 servers in Inner Mongolia, about 3,072 H100 and H200 units in Gansu
- Financing pattern: local government-backed ventures, universities and regional computing centers, with bank loans covering up to 80 percent in one case
- Already operating: an H800 cluster in Jiangsu reported in March 2025 and a Fujian H800 cluster disclosed by Damingcheng
- Caveat: tender documents do not prove delivery, and no link established to Megaspeed or to Huang's resignation
- Policy context: the administration is reviewing H200 export licenses case by case for eligible Chinese customers

## 4. Intel Opens a 23-Company Korean Alliance and Pitches an HBM-Free Inference GPU

### What Happened

At the 2026 Intel AI Summit Seoul, held at COEX, Intel Korea chief executive Bae Tae-won announced the K-AI Full Stack Alliance with 23 South Korean companies, aimed at building AI systems on Korean-designed servers. Intel said the event drew 52 companies including Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and LG Electronics plus AI chip startups FuriosaAI and DeepX, model developer Upstage, Nota AI, Megazone Cloud and server maker KTNF, and about 1,000 attendees. Intel also signed a memorandum of understanding with the Seoul AI Hub. On the hardware side, Anil Nanduri showed the physical D-Series chip, the GPU D270 code-named Crescent Island: 350 watts, up to 26,200 teraflops at FP4, and up to 480GB of LPDDR5X, the low-power DRAM used in phones, which Intel says is up to five times the 96GB on Nvidia's RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell Server Edition. Intel cited pre-production internal tests showing FP64 throughput 1.92 to 5.9 times comparable Nvidia parts on four financial simulations, 30 percent lower latency on Google's Gemma and 2.2 times faster agent execution, none independently verified, with no date or price given.

### Why It Matters

The D-Series is the first serious accelerator designed around the assumption that HBM, not compute, is the binding constraint. Trading bandwidth for capacity is the right call for long-document serving and high-concurrency agent loops, and wrong for prefill-heavy training, so it is a bet on inference shape rather than a general-purpose challenge. The Korean alliance matters more than the chip: pairing Korean servers, memory, NPUs and models with an Intel CPU and GPU stack gives Seoul a sovereign option that is neither Nvidia nor Chinese, at a moment when Nvidia just reaffirmed a 260,000-GPU supply plan to Korea. Intel's numbers are unverified and it still has no launch date, so treat this as a positioning announcement with a physical part, not a shipping product.

### Key Technical Points

- Alliance: K-AI Full Stack Alliance, 23 Korean companies, combining Korean server hardware with member chips, models and software for sovereign and industry-specific systems
- Named members: Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix, LG Electronics, FuriosaAI, DeepX, Upstage, Nota AI, Megazone Cloud, Dinotisia, Panesia, KTNF
- GPU D270 Crescent Island: 350W, up to 26,200 TFLOPS FP4, up to 480GB LPDDR5X, no HBM
- Capacity comparison: about five times the 96GB on Nvidia RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell Server Edition, at lower bandwidth
- Vendor claims, unverified: FP64 throughput 1.92 to 5.9 times Nvidia on four financial simulations, 30 percent lower latency on Gemma, 2.2 times faster agent execution
- Adjacent roadmap: Xeon Clearwater Forest with up to 288 efficiency cores in mass production, Diamond Rapids with up to 256 performance cores next year
- Status: no launch date and no price disclosed

## 5. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet for Agentic Payments

### What Happened

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet on November 13, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, and initially available through dots for ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older in the US with eligible US-issued cards. OpenAI describes it as the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to a user's actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase. A user adds a card once, then reviews items, total and selected card before approving each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint scan; the approval creates an authorization record. After approval the payment network issues a payment token with its own number, security code and expiration date, so the merchant and the agent never see the real card. Eligible disputes and existing card rewards are preserved, and the user transacts directly with the business. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook covering business claiming, Web Bot Auth signed requests, WebMCP tools and coordination with fraud vendors including Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd and payment service providers including Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe.

### Why It Matters

Payments are the first high-stakes action layer where the agent, the user, the network and the merchant each hold a different piece of authority, and OpenAI's design puts the authorization decision at the network rather than in the model or the prompt. That is architecturally more conservative than agentic commerce pitches that let a model hold credentials, and it means the security properties inherit from card networks instead of from prompt discipline. The near-term friction will be on the merchant side, where bot defenses and risk engines currently treat agent traffic as fraud by default. The stated roadmap, full delegation with spending limits and pre-approved categories, is where the liability questions actually start.

### Key Technical Points

- Launch scope: dots inside ChatGPT, Pro subscribers 18+, US-issued cards, United States only
- Trust model: user approves each purchase with biometrics; network issues a token per transaction; card details never shared with business or agent
- Preserved properties: card rewards and eligible dispute protections carry over; user remains the merchant's customer
- Network partnerships: American Express, Mastercard, Visa
- Merchant tooling: Commerce Partner Playbook, business claim and product feed, Web Bot Auth signed requests, WebMCP tools, plugin actions for carts and availability
- Ecosystem coordination: fraud prevention with Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd; payment service providers Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe
- Stated direction: eventual full delegation with spending limits, purchase categories and time windows, pre-approved under enhanced safeguards

# Important Paper of the Day

## Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000x Faster than Looped Models

Looped models became the default answer for reasoning, with wins on Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Unique, but they are unstable, slow to train and expensive at inference. This paper argues that descending on a learned energy solves the same puzzles better and far cheaper. The authors build CRAM, a learned version of Hopfield and Tank's 1980s energy, and report 99.996 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and 100 percent on Maze-Unique test sets after 42 milliseconds and 132 milliseconds of training on a single GPU, at least a thousand times faster than looped models on these tasks, while matching those baselines at their highest accuracy using at least 282 times fewer inference FLOPs. Energy descent also buys early stopping, compute that scales with problem difficulty and interpretable weights that encode puzzle constraints. The authors turn this into a methodological warning: trivially solvable benchmarks like Sudoku and Maze should not be used to argue that looped reasoning scales.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17309

# Important GitHub Project

## HybridRobotics/supermimic

SuperMimic trains a humanoid whole-body motion tracker together with a single differentiable world model, built on mjlab and released under Apache 2.0. Two task identifiers cover generalized-coordinate and maximal-coordinate representations on the Unitree G1, with scripts for training the world model and policy and for playback, and the repository has drawn about 1,434 stars and 92 forks since it was created on November 10. The design point is that a learned differentiable simulator, rather than a hand-built one, sits in the training loop, so gradient signal flows from the motion objective back through dynamics instead of being approximated by sampling.

Link: https://github.com/HybridRobotics/supermimic

# Trend Observation

Three layers were repriced on the same day, and each one moved authority outward from the model. Payments went to the card network: ChatGPT Wallet decides whether an agent may spend by issuing a token per transaction, not by trusting the model to behave. Silicon went to memory: Intel's D-Series accepts lower bandwidth to carry 480GB of LPDDR5X, which is an explicit bet that the HBM shortage, not FLOPs, shapes inference deployments for the next two years. Enforcement went to end use: 23 billion yuan of Chinese projects built around restricted part numbers shows that blocking sales does not block designs, so the question becomes who operates the installed capacity. Meanwhile the two open releases of the week, Inkling 2 Small and the FBI's subject ARTEX, illustrate the same asymmetry from opposite sides: capability now ships faster than the verification attached to it, whether that is an unreported red-team report or an intrusion framework that anyone can rent.
