## 1. Alibaba Opens Qwen4-Max as a Token Plan Preview, Weights Still Absent

### What Happened

At around 10am Beijing time on November 11, the official Qwen account said Qwen 4 is coming and placed Qwen4-Max into a preview on Alibaba's Token Plan, with early access also available through QwenWork, Qoder, the Qwen app and Qwen Studio. According to Qwen Cloud documentation, qwen4-max-preview supports a 1M-token context and up to 393,216 output tokens, and is a cloud-only service. No model card named Qwen4 existed in the Qwen organization on Hugging Face that day, so there are no weights to download, and the preview runs until November 25.

### Why It Matters

This is the first real appearance of the Qwen 4 family, but what it unveils is a cadence of capability first and weights later: developers get to test in hosted endpoints and first-party products while parameter counts, benchmarks and checkpoints stay behind. For teams planning self-hosting, the newest Qwen weights that can actually be deployed remain the Qwen3.8 line, so Qwen 4 should not enter current capacity budgets. The polished numbers circulating around the launch are also a reminder about information hygiene in the release window.

### Key Technical Points

- Officially confirmed: Qwen4-Max preview live on Token Plan, with early access via QwenWork, Qoder, Qwen app and Qwen Studio
- Qwen Cloud docs: 1M-token context, up to 393,216 output tokens, cloud-only
- No weights, no official parameter count, no independent benchmarks, no per-token price (Token Plan subscription only)
- Claims circulating in secondary sources, including a 1.2T total with 56B active, a score of 58 on the intelligence index, 1 dollar input and 4 dollars output per million tokens, and Apache 2.0 weights by December 20, have no official confirmation and are not treated as fact here
- Preview ends November 25; whether it converts to general availability is unstated

## 2. Epoch: An Open-Weight Model Breaks Six Firefox Versions for About 1,000 Dollars Each

### What Happened

In its November 11 brief, Epoch AI published a controlled test in which the open-weight GLM 5.3 was pointed at patches that were public but had not yet reached users, and asked to reverse-engineer attacks from them. It produced working exploits for six versions of Firefox at roughly 1,000 dollars per target. The same procedure did not reproduce against Chrome, and the authors note that a stronger model or better prompting could change that result. The brief also records Claude Fable 5.2 at 174 on the Epoch Capabilities Index, above what the pre-Astra trend for reasoning models would have predicted.

### Why It Matters

It turns open-weight diffusion of attack capability from a qualitative worry into a measured unit price: about a thousand dollars per target, six versions, exploiting the gap between patch publication and rollout rather than a zero-day. Epoch points out that patch gaps exist across browsers, phone operating systems and router firmware, so the cost structure transfers to other targets. For defenders, this is the first concrete estimate of what an adversary pays to work a patch window that is fixed but not yet shipped.

### Key Technical Points

- Method: reverse-engineer exploits from published but not-yet-distributed patches, not zero-days
- Result: six Firefox versions exploited at about 1,000 dollars each; Chrome did not reproduce
- Model: open-weight GLM 5.3; Epoch expects stronger models or better prompting to widen the hit surface
- Exposure: patch gaps are common across browsers, mobile systems and router firmware
- Same brief: Fable 5.2 set an ECI record at 174; new AI compute appears to double roughly every nine months, with quarterly additions growing about 2.5x per year and Broadcom doubling TPU shipments in one quarter

## 3. CloudSek: Silent Lynx Used the Open-Source Hermes Agent Framework to Steal 2,000+ Tajik Files

### What Happened

Reuters reported on November 11 that Singapore-based CloudSek, in a report published that day, said a hacking group other researchers have tied to Kazakhstan used Hermes, an open-source framework for autonomous AI agents, to break into neighbouring Tajikistan and steal more than 2,000 national security-related documents. CloudSek uncovered the operation after the attackers inadvertently left a server exposed to the internet. The group, tracked as Silent Lynx, used Hermes for reconnaissance from around mid-August, entered Tajikistan's electronic document system a couple of weeks later, and the agent then wrote a custom program to download, organize and chunk the files for exfiltration.

### Why It Matters

This is a checkable sample of an espionage-grade operation outsourcing the intrusion to an agent, and the tool involved is a public framework rather than bespoke malware. CloudSek's description is that the human stated the objective while Hermes planned the attacks, wrote the rules, ran them, kept notes and produced reports; the exposed server still held the role prompt given to the agent. Unlike earlier commodity crime cases, this makes the assumption that open-source agent frameworks are ready-made attack infrastructure the default rather than a hypothesis.

### Key Technical Points

- Actor: Silent Lynx, tied to Kazakhstan by multiple researchers; Russian-language working, Kazakh business hours, residential Kazakh internet egress
- Tooling: Hermes, an open-source autonomous agent framework, not custom malware
- Division of labour: human sets the objective; agent handles reconnaissance, planning, rule writing, execution, note-taking and reporting
- Outcome: Tajikistan's electronic document system, more than 2,000 national security documents
- Exposure point: an unhardened server left on the public internet containing the system prompt and operating guidance
- Significance: the practice extends from financially motivated crime to state-linked espionage

## 4. CoreWeave Beats and Revives the Trade as Baird Cuts NVIDIA to Neutral, Target Down 60 Percent

### What Happened

CoreWeave reported 3.66 billion dollars of third-quarter revenue, up 168 percent year over year and more than 1 billion sequentially, ahead of the 3.56 billion consensus; loss per share of 1.27 dollars was narrower than the 1.46 expected. Full-year guidance rose to 13.0 to 13.3 billion dollars from 12 to 13 billion, revenue backlog stands at 132.5 billion, and the company said it expects 9 billion dollars of customer prepayments and deposits in the next three months alone. The stock rose about 25 percent intraday and the Nasdaq gained about 2 percent. The same day, Baird downgraded NVIDIA to Neutral from Outperform and cut its price target to 200 dollars from 500.

### Why It Matters

Two opposite signals landed on one day: demand-side evidence in the form of prepayments, backlog and a raised guide, and risk-side repricing in the form of Baird explicitly lowering its 2027 hyperscaler capex growth assumption to about 40 percent and citing uncertainty over whether the CEO remains through 2027. Baird is only the third of 64 covering analysts at hold or below, which says the disagreement is about governance and 2027 growth rather than about current compute orders.

### Key Technical Points

- CoreWeave Q3: 3.66 billion dollars revenue, up 168 percent year over year and over 1 billion sequentially; EPS -1.27 versus -1.46 expected
- Guidance: full year 13.0 to 13.3 billion dollars, up from 12 to 13 billion; backlog 132.5 billion; 9 billion in prepayments and deposits expected in the next three months

## 5. arXiv Took About 42,000 Submissions in October as the Author Cap Failed to Bite

### What Happened

Times Higher Education reported on November 11 that arXiv received roughly 42,000 new papers in October, with submissions continuing to rise despite a per-author monthly cap introduced in the summer. The piece notes that Alexander Gasnikov, rector of Innopolis University, appeared as the second most prolific co-author, named on 11 October submissions posted by eight different scholars; he attributed the surge to new AI tools that let his team solve a set of long-open problems. The same outlet has reported editors building AI systems that question authors about their understanding of their own submissions.

### Why It Matters

This is a supply-side number about AI-assisted research rather than another model launch. The cap targets individual output, but collaboration networks route around it, so total volume keeps climbing while screening costs shift onto editors, readers and downstream researchers. For anyone doing literature review or trend analysis it means search signal-to-noise is degrading; for platforms it raises the question of who absorbs the triage bill.

### Key Technical Points

- Scale: about 42,000 submissions in October, well above the same period a year earlier
- Policy: since summer, two submissions per author per month with a maximum of three under review
- Workaround: prolific authors distribute submissions as co-authors; one team was named on 11 October papers from eight scholars
- Attribution: interviewees credit GPT Astra and Claude Fable with helping solve long-open problems
- Knock-on effect: journals are piloting AI that interrogates authors about their own submissions

# Important Paper of the Day

## Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime

Daniel Lenton builds a lifelong stream out of the three ARC-AGI editions, where tasks return after dormant gaps with fresh instances while new tasks keep arriving. On each instance the learner may spend budget on demonstrations, submit answers, or act inside the harness, and incorrect submissions can be retried at a cost, with each instance scored by the share of that feedback budget consumed. The important design choice is that a learner may keep anything between instances, memory or weights, so memory-based and weight-based systems are finally scored on the same scale. After the stream the learner is frozen and tested on fresh instances of seen tasks to measure memorization and on unseen tasks to measure generalization; experiments compare a frozen-weight agent harness with and without persistence against two prompting floors, a program-induction method and Qwen3.5-9B weights trained to imitate GLM-5.3 Flash.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505

# Important GitHub Project

## ccch1mneyyy/dsh-Go

A mobile implementation that puts DeepSeek Harness in your pocket: the agent runs locally on the phone and can also drive your computer remotely. Written in Dart under AGPL-3.0, created October 25 and still being pushed on November 11, currently around 19,685 stars and 877 forks. What makes it worth attention is not another client but the move of a long-running agent from the desktop to a device you carry: session state, tool calls and the remote control channel to the computer all have to stay coherent on one link, which is precisely the part of the harness layer under the most discussion lately.

# Trend Observation

The common thread today is bookkeeping: each item converted something previously vague into a price or a count. One exploit costs a thousand dollars and comes out of an open-weight model, giving capability diffusion the shape of a purchase order. A state-linked espionage campaign can be reduced to an objective handed to an open-source framework, which removes the need to argue that agent frameworks are attack infrastructure. The capital split shows up as two readings of one earnings report: CoreWeave proves demand with prepayments and backlog while Baird prices governance separately by halving NVIDIA's target, and the two are not contradictory. Model releases keep running capability first and weights later, with Qwen4-Max shipping cloud-only, Kimi's checkpoints arriving twelve days late, and frontier and open tiers deliberately separated. The 42,000 October submissions show that an author cap cannot constrain a collaboration network. The thread to watch next is not who shipped a model but who is pricing these capabilities, on what basis, and who ends up holding the bill.
