## 1. OpenAI Puts GPT-6 and Codex Inside Google Cloud's Model Garden

### What Happened

On October 21 OpenAI and Google Cloud announced that GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, together with OpenAI's Codex agentic coding workflows, are rolling out in Google Cloud's Model Garden as a private preview for selected enterprise customers, with general availability promised in the coming weeks. Customers buy through Google Cloud Marketplace and can spend existing Google Cloud commitments; inference runs on dedicated Google Cloud infrastructure behind isolated gateways, prompts and outputs stay inside the customer's data perimeter and are not used for training. Codex CLI and Desktop can already point at the platform for inference, and availability inside Gemini for business and Google Antigravity is listed as coming soon. Zero Data Retention and Confidential Compute are not available at launch.

### Why It Matters

Distribution, not capability, was the constraint this week. OpenAI's models now sit on all three large US clouds, since AWS began hosting them in April after Microsoft's exclusive licence lapsed, and Google is advertising itself as the only hyperscaler that offers Gemini, Claude and GPT side by side. That converts model choice into a line item on an existing cloud contract instead of a new vendor relationship, and it puts Gemini in the awkward position of having to win enterprise workloads with its strongest competitor listed next to it. For buyers the practical effect is procurement leverage and a single invoice; for OpenAI it is reach without building its own channel.

### Key Technical Points

- Private preview: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Luna plus Codex workflows in Model Garden, from October 21
- Purchased via Google Cloud Marketplace against existing commitments; one consolidated Google Cloud invoice
- Inference on dedicated Google Cloud infrastructure behind hardened isolated gateways; prompts and outputs stay in the customer perimeter, no training use
- Zero Data Retention and Confidential Compute listed as coming soon, not available at launch
- Codex CLI and Desktop can already route inference to the platform; Gemini for business and Antigravity integrations pending
- Google claims it is the only hyperscaler offering the three leading closed models: Gemini 4 Argon, Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra
- OpenAI says Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner

## 2. Recursive Opens Flint: a 39.8B/2.5B Coding MoE Built by Its Own Research Machine

### What Happened

On October 21 Recursive Superintelligence, led by Richard Socher, released Flint, its first model: a 39.8B-parameter mixture-of-experts coding model with roughly 2.5B active parameters per token, 256 routed experts of which 6 are selected plus one shared expert, and a 262,144-token context window in the model card configuration. Weights are on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, quantizations go down to 11.4 GB, and the company reports more than 125 tokens per second on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro and more than 150 on a 64 GB NVIDIA RTX Spark at short contexts. Vendor-reported scores are 89.7% on SWE-bench Pro v2, 74.2% on SWE-bench Verified and 54.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1. Recursive says the model was built from scratch in under four months and that its automated research system, the Eureka Machine, worked on nearly every part of the stack, including architecture, quantization and kernels.

### Why It Matters

Two claims deserve separating. The small one is a strong local coding model: 6% activation ratio buys laptop-speed inference at a quality that vendors usually only report for cluster-class systems. The large one is authorship. If an automated research system really shaped architecture, quantization and kernels on a four-month schedule with a small team, then the competitive bottleneck shifts from headcount and compute to the throughput of the research loop itself, and every lab is now measuring the same thing. Independent re-runs matter here, because SWE-bench Pro v2 at 89.7% sits above the same model's SWE-bench Verified score, which is unusual enough to warrant a second look.

### Key Technical Points

- 39.8B total parameters, about 2.5B active per token (roughly 6%), 256 routed experts with 6 selected plus 1 shared
- 262,144 tokens in the model card config; blog mentions up to 512K with best results inside 256K
- Apache 2.0 weights on Hugging Face; quantizations down to 11.4 GB
- Vendor-reported: SWE-bench Pro v2 89.7%, SWE-bench Verified 74.2%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 54.9%
- 125+ tokens/sec on M4 Pro MacBook Pro, 150+ on 64 GB RTX Spark at short contexts
- Eureka Machine automated research system credited with architecture, quantization and kernel work; built in under four months
- Day-one support in llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM and SGLang, plus the open rec engine; free to try on OpenCode and Pi

## 3. llm-d 1.0 Ships Under CNCF: Cache-Aware Routing for Agent Fleets

### What Happened

The llm-d project released 1.0 on October 20 at PyTorch Conference North America, and it was the talk of AI infrastructure on October 21. Founded in 2025 by Red Hat, IBM, Google Cloud, CoreWeave and NVIDIA and now a CNCF Sandbox project, llm-d turns a fleet of vLLM and SGLang servers into one coordinated inference pool on Kubernetes. The 1.0 release adds cache-aware routing that tracks which replica already holds an agent's prefix, multi-tenant quality of service with traffic prioritisation, zero-downtime upgrades and stable APIs. Contributors report a blended serving cost near $0.11 per million tokens, 5 to 10x cost reduction on context-heavy agentic workloads, 3x output throughput and the ability to serve up to 3,000 concurrent coding agents with zero preemptions. On Ironwood TPU, prefix-aware routing plus host memory offload cut median time-to-first-token by more than 85% and raised throughput 2.4x versus a plain Kubernetes Service. Google Cloud is integrating it into GKE across TPU and NVIDIA GPU.

### Why It Matters

Agentic workloads broke the assumption that a request is one-shot: an agent's calls share a long prefix, arrive in bursts and must not be preempted halfway through a tool loop. llm-d 1.0 is the first hardened open release that treats prefix locality and preemption policy as first-class scheduling inputs rather than tuning knobs. That matters commercially: if a neutral, CNCF-governed pool can serve 3,000 concurrent agents at a blended $0.11 per million tokens, the margin on proprietary inference optimization shrinks and the differentiator moves to model quality and vertical integration.

### Key Technical Points

- Founded 2025 by Red Hat, IBM, Google Cloud, CoreWeave and NVIDIA; now CNCF Sandbox, 500+ contributors across 25+ organisations
- Cache-aware routing sends each agent's next call to the replica already holding its prefix
- Multi-tenant QoS with traffic prioritisation, zero-downtime upgrades, stable APIs
- Blended serving cost around $0.11 per million tokens; 5-10x cost reduction on context-heavy agentic workloads; 3x output throughput
- Up to 3,000 concurrent coding agents with zero preemptions
- Ironwood TPU result: prefix-aware routing plus host memory offload cut median TTFT by over 85%, throughput 2.4x versus plain Kubernetes Service
- Google Cloud integrating llm-d 1.0 into GKE across TPU and NVIDIA GPU; IBM Research cites 11.5x lower inference cost than an all-proprietary configuration

## 4. NVIDIA Turns Cell Towers Into AI Compute as T-Mobile Places First Live AI-RAN Call

### What Happened

At GTC Berlin on October 21 NVIDIA and Nokia said telecom operators are adding accelerated computing to centralised data centres, metro sites and individual cell sites, turning carrier networks into distributed AI compute. T-Mobile placed the world's first AI-RAN call on a live commercial network, running Nokia's anyRAN software on NVIDIA's Aerial RAN Computer; BT, Deutsche Telekom, Elisa and Orange are lined up for trials, with Elisa already completing Europe's first AI-RAN field trial and BT preparing live UK trials. Nearly 20 operators globally have announced AI-RAN collaborations on the stack, with commercial services targeted for 2027. On the same day NVIDIA introduced DSX MaxLPS, claiming identical token output on up to 30% less power, and DSX POD reference designs for European AI factories with up to 100% reduction in cooling water. Nscale's Iceland test showed 49% higher inference throughput within the same 264-kilowatt budget.

### Why It Matters

The AI buildout has been gated by land, power and interconnection, not by GPUs. Reusing carrier real estate and fibre moves compute physically closer to users and turns a sunk national asset into an inference footprint, which is a materially different answer to the capacity problem than another greenfield campus. It also reframes the telco business: an operator that already owns the tower, the power drop and the backhaul can sell latency rather than bandwidth. The catch is scheduling, because radio traffic is non-negotiable and AI work must yield to it, which is exactly what MaxLPS and the flexible-power work are for.

### Key Technical Points

- T-Mobile placed the world's first AI-RAN call on a live commercial network using Nokia anyRAN on NVIDIA Aerial RAN Computer
- Trials at BT, Deutsche Telekom, Elisa and Orange; Elisa completed Europe's first AI-RAN field trial; nearly 20 operators announced AI-RAN collaborations
- Compute deployed at three tiers: centralised data centres, metro sites and individual cell sites
- Runs AI workloads alongside radio signal processing on GPU-accelerated rather than fixed-function radio silicon
- DSX MaxLPS claims the same token output on up to 30% less power; Nscale Iceland test showed 49% higher throughput in the same 264 kW budget
- DSX POD reference designs for European AI factories with up to 100% cooling water reduction
- Commercial AI-RAN services targeted for 2027

## 5. LiteLLM 1.107.0 Ships a Credential Stealer From Its Own Release Pipeline

### What Happened

On October 21, version 1.107.0 of LiteLLM, an LLM gateway library downloaded tens of millions of times a week, was published to PyPI and to the project's Docker tags with malware inside. All seven wheels, covering Linux, macOS and Windows, planted three files, cook.pth, cook.py and sitecustomize.py, into site-packages; both payloads execute when any Python process starts on the host, even one that never imports litellm. The stealer collects SSH keys, cloud credentials, CI/CD secrets, browser passwords, shell history, AI tool tokens and LiteLLM proxy provider keys, and sends them to www.litellm[.]dev, while a daemon polls a GitHub-hosted domain file hourly and executes whatever code it returns. The upstream GitHub source does not contain the files, and PyPI has removed 1.107.0; 1.106.2 and the 1.107.0 release candidates are clean. SafeDep assesses with moderate to high confidence that BerriAI's own private release pipeline built the wheels from an attacker-modified git source, noting no provenance attestations and no matching public GitHub Actions run.

### Why It Matters

This is the second LiteLLM supply-chain incident this year, and the vector is the interesting part: the published artifact diverged from the audited source, so ordinary dependency review would have passed. LiteLLM is exactly the component that holds every model provider's API key in one place, which makes it a higher-value target than a generic package and explains why the payload searched for AI tool credentials specifically. The practical rule for teams is to stop treating model gateways as ordinary dependencies: pin them, verify provenance attestations where they exist, and assume that any host which ran this version has leaked every secret it could read.

### Key Technical Points

- Seven wheels (Linux, macOS, Windows) published to PyPI between 11:39:51 and 11:40:13 UTC on 21 October 2026
- Planted cook.pth, cook.py and sitecustomize.py in site-packages; payloads run at Python startup, no import of litellm required
- Exfiltration target www.litellm[.]dev; harvested SSH keys, cloud credentials, CI/CD secrets, browser passwords, shell history, AI tool tokens and proxy provider keys
- Backdoor daemon polls a GitHub-hosted domain file hourly and executes returned code
- Docker tags v1.107.0, latest, main-latest and main-stable also affected
- Upstream GitHub source clean; PyPI removed 1.107.0; 1.106.2 and the 1.107.0 release candidates are safe
- No CVE assigned; no provenance attestations on the affected releases; second LiteLLM supply-chain incident in 2026 after March

# Important Paper of the Day

## The Virtual Biotech: a Multi-Agent AI Framework for Therapeutic Discovery and Development

Published in Science on October 21 (a bioRxiv preprint from February), the Virtual Biotech is a Stanford framework in which a chief-scientific-officer agent delegates to specialised scientist agents across genetics, omics, chemoinformatics and clinical data, with a reviewer agent checking the work. In one showcase, 37,075 clinical-trialist agents curated outcomes from Phase 2 and 3 trials across 55,984 trials in six hours, a job the authors estimate would take a single agent 76.6 days; agent annotations matched human reviewers 89.7% of the time on primary endpoints. Case studies proposed a B7-H3 antibody-drug-conjugate strategy for lung cancer and surfaced failure modes for a terminated ulcerative colitis trial. The authors stress that a single error can propagate through the system and that human expert involvement remains essential; these are agent-curated observational associations, not wet-lab proof.

Link: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.23.707551

# Important GitHub Project

## google-research/harness2

Harness² is Google Research's open framework for "recursive agent harnessing in an open world", released under Apache 2.0 at the start of October, with an accompanying paper on arXiv (2610.14224). It treats the harness itself, meaning prompts, tools, memory, skills and orchestration, as the object of study rather than an implementation detail, and ships a runnable benchmark path: install with uv, run the JobBench setup, then execute a parallel sweep at k=1. At 17,184 stars and 1,594 forks, the repository gained roughly 16,000 stars in a week and topped GitHub's Python trending list on October 21, which is unusually fast for research code. The signal is that harness portability and harness evaluation are becoming a shared vocabulary, one week after Google's own agent-safety work and five days after two labs shipped harness-agnostic tooling of their own.

Link: https://github.com/google-research/harness2

# Trend Observation

Three of today's five items are about who owns the layer between a model and a workload. Google letting OpenAI models onto its own shelf, llm-d hardening the inference pool, and NVIDIA pushing compute into base stations are all moves to own the place where requests are routed rather than the model that answers them. The LiteLLM compromise is the same layer seen from the other side: it is precisely the routing component that concentrates every provider key in one process, which is why it was worth attacking twice in a year. The pattern is consistent with the shift from the last two weeks: capability gains are being commoditised while routing, cache locality, power budgets and provenance become the scarce, defensible and attackable assets.
