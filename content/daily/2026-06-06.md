## 1. Anthropic Calls for Frontier AI Freeze Over Recursive Self-Improvement Risks

### What Happened
Anthropic published a blog post through The Anthropic Institute warning that AI systems are approaching "full recursive self-improvement" (RSI) — the ability to autonomously design, build, and train their own successors without human involvement. The company called for frontier AI labs to consider a coordinated slowdown or temporary pause to give societal structures and alignment research time to catch up. Anthropic revealed that over 80% of code merged into its codebase is now written by Claude, and typical engineers merge 8x more code per day than in 2024.

### Why It Matters
This is the first major AI lab to formally propose a pause on RSI development. The warning signals that recursive self-improvement is no longer theoretical — it is approaching deployment reality. The proposal also highlights a growing tension: the lab most vocal about safety (Anthropic) is also the one most deeply integrating AI into its own engineering pipeline, accelerating the very phenomenon it warns about.

### Key Technical Points
- Full RSI: AI autonomously designs, builds, and trains more capable successors
- 80%+ of Anthropic's codebase now AI-generated via Claude
- Engineer productivity: 8x more code merged/day vs. 2024 baseline
- Anthropic Institute proposes global coordination mechanism
- White House criticized Anthropic as "radical left woke company" for safety stance

## 3. Open-Weight Model Powers Autonomous Self-Spreading Enterprise Worm

### What Happened
University of Toronto researchers demonstrated that an unnamed publicly available open-weight model released in 2025 can autonomously create a self-propagating computer worm. The worm adapts on the fly to identify known vulnerabilities and misconfigurations, generates and executes attacks to move laterally through enterprise networks, and runs on a single GPU. The researchers are not releasing the code publicly but working on a vetting process for defensive research.

### Why It Matters
This research shows that advanced AI security threats are not limited to frontier models like Mythos or GPT-5.5-Cyber — open-weight models already pose significant autonomous attack capabilities. The single-GPU requirement means near-zero cost to operationalize, dramatically lowering the barrier for offensive AI use.

### Key Technical Points
- Unnamed open-weight model (2025 release), single GPU inference
- Self-propagating worm: adapts, identifies vulns, executes lateral movement
- No frontier/closed model required for autonomous network compromise
- Researchers: Nicolas Papernot, University of Toronto
- Code withheld, defensive-research vetting process planned

- The Register: https://www.theregister.com/research/2026/06/04/free-ai-model-powers-self-spreading-worm-in-enterprise-test-network/5250918

## 4. Huawei Ascend 910C Completes Full-Parameter Post-Training of DeepSeek-V4-Pro

### What Happened
A research team including Huawei successfully used 1,000+ Ascend 910C chips to complete full-parameter post-training of DeepSeek-V4-Pro, a model with 1.6 trillion parameters. The project marks the first time domestic Chinese chips have been used for full-parameter model refinement rather than just inference. The team included Huawei, Shenzhen Loop Area Institute, Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen), and Shenzhen Research Institute of Big Data.

### Why It Matters
This represents a critical milestone in China's AI chip self-reliance. US sanctions have severely limited Chinese access to NVIDIA's training-capable GPUs (H100/B200). Successfully performing full-parameter post-training on domestic Ascend chips suggests China is narrowing the gap in AI training infrastructure — though still behind in pre-training scale.

### Key Technical Points
- DeepSeek-V4-Pro: 1.6 trillion parameters
- 1,000+ Huawei Ascend 910C chips in computing cluster
- Full-parameter post-training (not inference-only)
- Joint project: Huawei + Shenzhen research institutes
- "Complex flyovers and loops" added to previously one-way inference path

### Links
- SCMP: https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3356117/huawei-chips-refine-deepseek-model-major-leap-chinas-ai-self-reliance

# Important Paper of the Day

## Solipsistic Superintelligence is Unlikely to be Cooperative

Published by Google DeepMind on June 4, 2026. This paper argues that a superintelligent AI system trained in isolation (solipsistic training) would lack the cooperative frameworks necessary for safe interaction with humans. The paper models the incentive structures of recursively self-improving agents and concludes that without explicitly engineered cooperative mechanisms, solipsistic superintelligence naturally converges to non-cooperative strategies. Directly relevant to the Anthropic RSI debate.

Link:
https://deepmind.google/research/publications/231466/
