## 1. Google Signs Up-to-$12.2B Custom AI Chip Deal with Marvell

### What Happened
Marvell and Google announced an expanded commercial agreement for custom AI chips, including AI inference accelerators, networking and memory products. As part of the deal, Google receives warrants to buy up to 58.97 million Marvell shares at $206.58 apiece — worth up to $12.2B — with most vesting based on cumulative custom chip purchases through fiscal 2033. Marvell stock jumped 12% on the news, while Broadcom fell about 5% as its exclusive Google custom silicon relationship (signed through 2031) faces its first real challenger.

### Why It Matters
This completes the hyperscaler custom silicon sweep: Marvell now has a foothold at every major cloud player. The equity-warrant structure is unusual for a supplier deal and signals deepening financial interdependence between chip vendors and hyperscalers. For Google, dual-sourcing TPU-class silicon reduces concentration risk; for Broadcom, it ends a de facto monopoly on Google's most strategic AI hardware.

### Key Technical Points
- Warrants: up to 58.97M shares at $206.58, worth up to $12.2B
- Full vesting requires Google to spend ~$120B cumulatively through FY2033
- Scope: AI inference accelerators, networking, memory products
- Broadcom's Google agreement runs through 2031 — now non-exclusive in practice
- Marvell's optical interconnect (PAM4 DSPs) segment guided to grow 70%+ YoY in FY2027

## 2. Cerebras Unveils CS-4: 30x Faster Inference Than GPUs

### What Happened
Cerebras introduced the CS-4, its fourth-generation rack-scale system built from three new Wafer Scale Engine 3 Turbo (WSE-3T) processors — the first product on the new Nexus platform architecture. Each WSE-3T packs 4 trillion transistors and 900,000 AI-optimized cores across 46,225 mm² of silicon with 44GB of on-wafer SRAM. The CS-4 delivers 750 PFLOPS of AI compute, 129.6 PB/s memory bandwidth, and supports models over 50 trillion parameters. Cerebras claims up to 30x faster AI inference than GPU-based systems and 10x the throughput per watt.

### Why It Matters
Cerebras is pushing wafer-scale compute from a niche into the mainstream inference race, directly challenging Nvidia's economics at the token-generation layer — the same layer where Cerebras already powers OpenAI's "Ultrafast" mode. The redesigned Nexus architecture with modular "Wafer-Scale Backpacks" cuts component count by 50% and deployment time from days to hours, addressing the manufacturing-scale criticisms that have dogged wafer-scale approaches.

### Key Technical Points
- WSE-3T: 4T transistors, 900K cores, 44GB SRAM, 46,225 mm², 250 PFLOPS each
- CS-4: 3× WSE-3T, 750 PFLOPS, 129.6 PB/s memory bandwidth, 7.2 Tb/s I/O
- Wafer-to-wafer latency as low as 2 microseconds
- Benchmark: 4,400+ tokens/s/user on a 120B-parameter model
- Nexus architecture: power conversion moved 100x closer to processors
- 50% fewer components, 60% more automated manufacturing

## 3. SpaceX Tried to Acquire AI Coding Startup Cognition — and Was Rebuffed

### What Happened
Bloomberg reported that SpaceX approached Cognition, the startup behind AI coding agent Devin, with an acquisition offer — just five days after closing its $60B purchase of Cursor. Cognition's CEO denied the report publicly, and the startup is reportedly in early talks for a new funding round at a $40B valuation, up from its $25B post-money valuation in May. Cognition is now one of the largest independent AI coding companies not yet absorbed by a major player.

### Why It Matters
The episode reveals the intensity of the AI coding land grab: SpaceX has now targeted both Cursor and Cognition within a single week, and the $40B+ price tag reflects how the market values frontier coding agents. Cognition's refusal signals that top-tier AI software startups still believe independent scale beats acquisition — even from a nearly $2.3T-market-cap buyer. It also underscores SpaceX's aggressive diversification from space into AI compute and software.

### Key Technical Points
- Cognition: maker of Devin, autonomous AI coding agent, founded 2023
- May 2026: $1B round at $25B post-money valuation
- New round reportedly in early talks at $40B valuation
- SpaceX acquired Cursor for $60B (closed mid-August 2026)
- SpaceX acquired xAI earlier in 2026; IPO in June peaked near $2.3T market cap

The move also signals Nvidia's strategy of taking equity positions across the full AI stack, from chips to data to inference clouds.

## 5. Arm Beats Q2 Forecasts as AGI Data Center CPU Demand Exceeds $2B

### What Happened
Arm Holdings beat quarterly revenue and earnings estimates with a stronger-than-expected revenue forecast, driven by robust AI infrastructure demand. The company said customer demand for its in-house AGI data center CPU now exceeds $2B across fiscal 2027-2028, and the processor has already shipped to multiple customers. It adds another front to the custom silicon war, and the smartphone royalty softness hints that Arm's growth narrative is now decisively AI-driven rather than mobile-driven.

# Important Paper of the Day

## The HydroGym Reinforcement Learning Platform for Fluid Dynamics

Published in Nature (August 2026), HydroGym is an open reinforcement learning platform designed for fluid dynamics control problems. Developed by researchers including Christian Lagemann, Ricardo Vinuesa and Steven L. Brunton, it provides a standardized, reproducible environment for training RL agents to control fluid flows — a class of problems with enormous engineering impact (drag reduction, energy efficiency, aerodynamic design). By bridging deep RL with computational fluid dynamics, HydroGym addresses the reproducibility gap that has hindered RL research in scientific domains.

Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10917-6
