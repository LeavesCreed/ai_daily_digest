## 1. Alphabet Raises $80B for AI Infrastructure, Berkshire Invests $10B

### What Happened
Alphabet announced its first equity offering in over 20 years, raising $80 billion through a $30B underwritten stock offering, $40B at-the-market program, and a $10B strategic investment from Berkshire Hathaway — Warren Buffett's conglomerate's largest single AI bet. Funds will expand Google's AI compute infrastructure. Alphabet guided 2026 capex at $180-190B with expectations of significant increases in 2027.

### Why It Matters
This is one of the largest equity fundraisings in history and signals that even the world's largest tech companies require external capital to fund the AI infrastructure buildout. Berkshire's $10B entry validates AI infrastructure as a long-term capital-intensive asset class. The scale ($180-190B 2026 capex) resets expectations for AI infrastructure spending industry-wide.

### Key Technical Points
- First Alphabet stock offering since 2004 IPO
- $30B underwritten offering + $40B at-the-market program + $10B Berkshire private placement
- Berkshire purchased 14.2M Class A shares at $351.81 and 14.4M Class C shares at $348.20
- 2026 capex guided at $180-190B, with 2027 expected to "significantly increase"
- Proceeds also used for tax obligations on employee equity vesting

## 2. Sam Altman Visits Washington: OpenAI Diverges from White House on AI Safety Rules

### What Happened
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman met with White House officials and bipartisan Congressional leadership including House Speaker Mike Johnson and Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. OpenAI publicly diverged from Trump's new AI Executive Order on two key points: opposing mandatory government approval before model releases and raising concerns about classified benchmarking by intelligence agencies. Separately, French President Macron invited Altman to attend the G7 Leaders Summit later this month.

### Why It Matters
This represents the first major public split between a frontier AI lab and the Trump administration's AI regulatory framework. OpenAI's alternative proposal — expanding voluntary testing at the Department of Commerce rather than classified NSA-led benchmarking — could reshape how the US government oversees frontier model releases. Altman's G7 invitation signals AI governance is becoming a top-tier diplomatic priority.

### Key Technical Points
- Altman advocates against proposals requiring government approval before model release
- OpenAI wants expanded Commerce Department AI testing with cybersecurity/biological weapons experts
- Concerns about NSA-led classified benchmarking creating opacity for companies
- Altman invited to G7 Leaders Summit by Macron
- OpenAI also pushing for increased federal AI testing funding

## 3. Broadcom Q2: AI Chip Revenue Doubles to $10.8B, But Forecast Unchanged

### What Happened
Broadcom reported fiscal Q2 earnings with AI semiconductor revenue doubling year-over-year to $10.8B, driven by custom AI chip demand from six core customers including Anthropic, Google, Meta, and OpenAI. However, CEO Hock Tan did not raise the full-year AI chip sales target of $100B, and software revenue missed expectations, causing the stock to decline 5% in after-hours trading.

### Why It Matters
Broadcom's unchanged full-year forecast despite AI revenue doubling suggests potential capacity constraints or customer diversification slowing down. As the second-largest AI chip company after NVIDIA, Broadcom's results provide a critical temperature check on the AI hardware buildout. The revelation of six core custom chip customers (including Anthropic and OpenAI as confirmed names) highlights the concentration risk in AI chip demand.

### Key Technical Points
- Q2 AI semiconductor revenue: $10.8B (more than double YoY)
- Full-year AI chip sales target unchanged at $100B
- Six core custom chip customers: Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI + two undisclosed
- Software segment weakness drove the stock decline
- Stock dropped ~5% after hours on unchanged guidance

## 4. EU Parliament Switches from Google to French Search Engine Qwant

### What Happened
The European Parliament announced it will replace Google with French search engine Qwant as the default on all parliamentary computers starting June 4, 2026. The move is part of the EU's "Buy and Use European" digital sovereignty strategy. The European Commission simultaneously announced new measures on chips, cloud computing services, and AI, including minimum energy-efficiency standards for data centres.

### Why It Matters
This is the most tangible institutional rejection of US big tech by the EU government itself. While symbolic in immediate impact, it signals that EU institutions are willing to act on digital sovereignty. The accompanying data centre energy efficiency standards directly impact the AI infrastructure buildout in Europe, where power constraints are already a growing concern.

### Key Technical Points
- Qwant replaces Google as default search on Parliament's Edge and Firefox browsers
- Part of EU "Buy and Use European" initiative
- EU Commission announces measures on chips, cloud, AI simultaneously
- Minimum energy-efficiency standards for data centres to be developed
- Follows EU sovereign AI infrastructure push (SoftBank €45B France investment)

## 5. Anthropic Expands Claude Mythos to Critical Infrastructure Across 15+ Countries

### What Happened
Anthropic expanded Project Glasswing, adding ~150 new organizations across 15+ countries to access Claude Mythos Preview for vulnerability scanning. New members include Samsung, SK Hynix, SK Telecom, NATO, EU cyber agency ENISA, and Okta — spanning power, water, healthcare, communications, and hardware sectors previously not represented in the initial 50-partner cohort.

### Why It Matters
Claude Mythos represents the most capable AI cybersecurity model ever demonstrated, having discovered 10,000+ zero-day vulnerabilities in initial testing. Expanding access to critical infrastructure operators is a high-stakes experiment: it could dramatically improve infrastructure security, but also demonstrates the dual-use risk of frontier AI for offensive cyber operations. Anthropic expects competitors to match Mythos capability soon, hence the rush to establish safeguards.

### Key Technical Points
- Expanded from 50 initial partners to ~200 total organizations
- Sectors added: power grid, water treatment, healthcare systems, telecoms, hardware
- Key new members: Samsung, SK Hynix, NATO, ENISA, Okta
- Anthropic also exploring Mythos for patching open-source vulnerabilities
- Company racing to establish norms before competitors release equivalent models

# Important Paper of the Day

## Reasoning-Empowered Task-Oriented Communication for Agent Networks

Published in Nature Communications. This paper proposes a fundamentally new communication framework for multi-agent AI systems, where communication becomes a reasoning-driven process rather than a bandwidth-optimized one. Agents autonomously determine not only what information to transmit, but when and why to communicate — optimizing for joint task execution rather than data throughput. The framework is critical infrastructure for the emerging "agent networks" paradigm where distributed AI agents must collaborate across physical and digital domains.

# Important GitHub Project

## Microsoft Intelligent Terminal

Microsoft open-sourced under MIT license a fork of Windows Terminal with native AI agent integration. Features include an agent status bar, a context-aware agent pane that docks within the terminal, automatic error detection on commands, and configurable support for different AI agent CLIs. Represents Microsoft's vision of AI-native developer tooling, though currently Windows-only.
